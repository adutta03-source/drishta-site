Is this for you?

Nobody signs up for “metacognition”.
They sign up at 2am.

Something keeps repeating. Or something is about to change. See if one of these sounds familiar.

When your mind won’t let go

  • “I can’t switch my brain off.”The 1am replay.
  • “Everyone’s ahead of me.”The scroll that ends in envy.
  • “I’m exhausted and don’t care.”Running on empty.
  • “Why did I say that again?”The reply you’d take back.
  • “I absorb everyone’s problems.”Holding everyone. Held by no one.

When the road splits

  • “I don’t know if this is my life.”The fork in the road.
  • “New job, new city, new year.”The clean slate you don’t want to waste.

When enough isn’t enough

  • “It’s going well. Why don’t I feel it?”Everything on paper. Nothing in the chest.
  • “I want to understand my own mind.”The curious one.

Drishta isn’t therapy. It’s training. For people who are fine on paper and not quite free inside. If you’re in crisis, or working through something serious, please start with a professional; we’re glad to point you to one. You don’t need to have meditated before.

Our philosophy
draṣṭā
drush-taaSanskrit · the seer, the one who witnesses

Learn to see your mind, not be ruled by it.

Long before science studied it, yoga gave it a name.

Drishta takes its name from Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra, the foundational text of classical yoga. There, the draṣṭā is the seer: the part of you that can watch thought and feeling without being carried away by them. Today, cognitive science calls this ability metacognition, and links it to better learning, clearer decisions and steadier emotions.

Drishta is a community built to practise it together. In immersive gatherings in nature, rooted in an ancient path and grounded in modern science, witnessing becomes a skill you can learn, share with others and carry into everyday life.

We were practising it for years before we knew its name.

The method

One loop. Practised until it’s yours.

We call it the Drishta Loop. Every practice runs the same three moves. It starts in the body, because the body knows first. It always ends in a choice, so looking inward never turns into going round in circles.

  1. Witness

    Catch it as it happens. The tight jaw, the racing chest, the story starting up.

    Science: monitoring, meta-awareness, interoception · Patañjali: draṣṭā, the seer

  2. Analyse

    Name what it is, not why it is. “This is fear,” not “what’s wrong with me?”

    Science: affect labelling, decentering · Patañjali: viveka, discernment

  3. Realise

    See it clearly, then pick your response. The space between the two is where you get your life back.

    Science: reappraisal, reduced reactivity · Patañjali: pratipakṣa-bhāvanam

Five places to practise it

BodyWhere it shows up first. Check-ins, slow walking, “where do I feel this?”
EmotionFinding the exact word for what you feel, and riding the wave.
ThoughtWatching thoughts arrive without boarding them. “I’m having the thought that…”
RelationshipsStaying yourself in a room full of other people. Pairs, circles, compassion.
Nature and worldAttention that widens. Awe walks, sitting with trees, small acts of service.

Every session has the same shape. Arrive in the body. Practise one loop. Reflect. Share. Carry one small practice home.

heyaṃ duḥkham anāgatam

“The suffering that has not yet come can be avoided.” Yoga Sūtra 2.16

That is what Drishta is for. Not repair after the fall: training before it. And one rule for how we hold each other: detached from capture, not from care.

The founding retreat

Three days to train the seer.

Our first retreat. A small group, a quiet place in the foothills, a drive from Delhi. No performance, no pressure to share. You choose how deep you go, at every step, and you leave with a practice that still works on a Tuesday.

Fri 13 – Sun 15 November 2026

The Drishta Retreat

  • Day 1 · SeeingArrive, settle, and begin with the body. Learn to name what you feel. Close the day in circle. “I can notice.”
  • Day 2 · UnderstandingA half-day of gentle silence and a slow walk. Watch thought at work, practise compassion in pairs, make something with your hands, end by the fire. “I am not my thoughts.”
  • Day 3 · CarryingWalk for awe, sit with trees, give something back. Then design the daily practice you’ll take home, and meet the buddy who’ll keep you to it. “I can do this at home.”

Before you come: a short form so we know you, one question to set your intention, one practice to begin with. After you leave: a check-in in week one, five-minute daily practices, your practice buddy and your first monthly Circle.

Who’ll be with you

Akanshya Dutta guides the method and the circles. Dr Partha Pratim Das shapes the creative practice and the space. A certified breath and body facilitator joins us, and our venue hosts us as a partner.

How we keep it safe

We get to know you before you arrive. Every practice has a gentler version and a way out: eyes open or closed, sit or walk, join in or sit out. Grounding is always at hand, and if you need more than we offer, we know who to send you to.

How you’ll know it worked

Short, research-based check-ins before, right after and a month later. We would rather show you what changed than promise it.

Where, and what it costs

Why Drishta

Deeper than an app. Gentler than Vipassana.

Ancient roots, checked against science. A community, not a guru. Built for before things break, not after.

Meditation apps

Good at calm, but you’re alone with a screen, and most people stop. At Drishta, there are people who notice when you don’t show up.

Ten-day silent retreats

Profound, and hard to fit around a job. Drishta brings the same kind of seeing into a weekend, inside the life you already have.

Ashrams and yoga retreats

Rich in tradition, often devotional or only about the body. Drishta is secular and method-first, with the science sitting beside the source.

Wellness resorts

Rest without insight. The calm checks out when you do. Drishta gives you a skill you still have on Monday morning.

Therapy and coaching

Essential when something is wrong. Drishta is for prevention and growth, and we’ll point you to a professional when you need one.

Grounded in research

The research, briefly.

  • Almost halfof our waking hours, the mind is somewhere else, and we are less happy for it (Killingsworth and Gilbert, 2010).
  • Name it to calm itPutting a feeling into words quietened the brain’s alarm centre (Lieberman et al., 2007).
  • 90 minutesof walking in nature eased rumination, where a city walk did not (Bratman et al., 2015).

So we’ll say train, practise, notice. We won’t say heal, cure or rewire. The effects of practices like these are real and modest, and they last only if you keep practising. That’s why Drishta is a community, not a one-off.

Questions people ask

Before you decide.

Is this spiritual or religious?

No. We read Patañjali the way you’d read an old map that still turns out to be accurate. No rituals, no beliefs to adopt, no chanting.

Is there a guru?

No. Facilitators hold the space; your own experience is the teacher. Opt out of anything, any time, no explanation needed.

Is this therapy?

No. It’s training for everyday life. If you’re carrying something heavy, please talk to a professional first; we’re glad to suggest where to start.

I’ve never meditated. Can I come?

Yes. Practices start short, start in the body, and every one has an easier version.

What if I can’t afford it?

Start free, or with a Drishta Evening. Every retreat also holds scholarship seats, paid for by our corporate work.
