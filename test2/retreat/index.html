Is this you?

Something this year made your own mind feel like the problem.

  • It's 1 a.m. and I'm still rewriting a reply to my manager in my head.

    Work is over, but your mind isn't. You rest and feel guilty. You scroll to switch off and wake up tired.

  • I've been deciding whether to quit for eight months.

    You're stuck at a fork: a job, a city, a relationship, an exam. Sunday evenings are the worst.

  • I know exactly why I do it. I still do it.

    Therapy, books or journaling showed you the pattern. Catching it while it happens is another matter.

  • I don't want to rebuild the same life that burnt me out.

    After burnout, a layoff or a breakup, you want to understand your own part in it before you start again.

  • I'm surrounded by people and still lonely.

    New city, far from home, hungry for hills, trees and people who think the way you do.

None of these is a character flaw. They're what an untrained mind does under pressure. Watching it is a skill, and it can be learned.

What you leave with

Three things you can use on Monday.

  • A mind you can calm

    Notice the spiral as it starts, and choose how to respond instead of being carried by it. Useful at 1 a.m., and in the meeting that sets you off.

  • Deeper focus

    Less drifting, more absorption. Through making things by hand, you'll feel what it's like when attention settles, and learn how to get back there.

  • A circle in your city

    Eleven people who did this with you, and a small group back in Delhi that keeps practising. The retreat is the start, not the holiday.

Why it works

The skill has a name: metacognition.

Metacognition means thinking about your own thinking: noticing what your mind is doing while it does it. Cognitive science links it to clearer decisions, steadier emotions and better learning. Long before that, Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra called the one who watches the draṣṭā, the seer. That's where Drishta's name comes from.

Most retreats sell rest. Rest helps until Monday. Drishta teaches the skill underneath, so it comes home with you. Every activity follows the same simple loop:

  1. PredictGuess how you'll react before you begin.
  2. DoWalk, make, write or sit in silence.
  3. NoticeCatch what your mind actually did.
  4. AdjustTry a different response, and carry it back.
The three days

Quiet days, small circles, hands in the clay.

FridayArrive · To See

  • Shared drive from Delhi in the morning. Phones go in a basket on arrival, if you choose.
  • Settle in and walk the land.
  • First circle: what brought you here.
  • Evening: ten minutes of watching your own mind, then writing down what it did.

SaturdayTo Feel · To Realise

  • Sunrise sit.
  • Attention walk in silence: notice where your attention goes.
  • Silent clay: making with your hands, and watching the mind while you do.
  • Writing and small circles: the patterns you've noticed and where they began.
  • Evening by the fire, mostly unscheduled.

SundayTo Connect · To Live

  • Pair practice: listening without fixing.
  • Your one practice for Monday, written in your own words.
  • Closing circle, then the drive back to Delhi by night.

At least a third of each day stays unscheduled. Every activity is an invitation: you can sit any of them out without explaining.

To be clear

What this is not, and how we look after you.

It isn't

  • Therapy. It's a practice. If you're in crisis right now, we'll gently point you to better help first.
  • Religious or spiritual. No rituals, no gurus. We borrow one word from an old text and the rest from science.
  • A performance. No one is asked to share more than they want to.
  • A spa weekend. You'll rest, but you'll also practise.

We make sure

  • A facilitator trained in mental health first aid, plus a second trained person, so no one is ever alone with a hard moment.
  • A counsellor on call by phone for all three days.
  • A short intake chat before you come, so we know what you need.
  • No photos of faces unless you say yes.

Questions

What people ask before they come.

Do I have to talk about personal things in front of strangers?

No. Circles are small and you share only what you choose. Many of the practices are done alone, in silence or with your hands. Saying "pass" is always fine.

I've never meditated. Is that a problem?

Not at all. There's no posture to master and nothing to believe. If you can walk, write and notice what you're thinking, you can do this.

Will I have to give up my phone?

We invite you to leave it in a basket for most of the day. It's your choice, and there's signal for emergencies.

I'm going through something heavy right now. Should I come?

Tell us in the intake chat. If a retreat isn't the right step yet, we'll say so honestly and help you find support, and you'll get a full refund.

How do I get there?

We travel together from Delhi in a shared vehicle on Friday morning and return Sunday night. If you'd rather come by train, we'll arrange a pickup from the nearest station.

What's the food like?

Simple, home-style, mostly vegetarian, at fixed times. Tell us about any allergies or needs when you register.
