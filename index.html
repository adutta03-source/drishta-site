The name
draṣṭā
Sanskrit · the seer, the one who witnesses

The part of you that watches.

In Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra, the draṣṭā is the seer: the one who watches thought and feeling without being carried off by them. Drishta takes its name from that word.

Most people are never given the time or the language to observe their inner world. Drishta makes that observation a learnable, measurable skill.

The approach

Four movements, practised in order.

Metacognition is the capacity to think about one's own thinking. Every Drishta programme follows four movements, delivered through dialogue, making, time in nature and silence.

  1. Witness

    Observe thought, emotion and sensation as they arise, without reacting.

  2. Reflect

    Examine where a reaction comes from and what it signals.

  3. Understand

    Recognise patterns in yourself, in others and in the systems you live within.

  4. Grow

    Choose a considered response and carry it into work, relationships and community.
